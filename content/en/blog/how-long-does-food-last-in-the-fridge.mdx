How long does food last in the fridge? It depends on the item — raw ground meat is a day or two, hard cheese can be months — and on one number: your fridge temperature. Below is a storage chart built from the USDA / FDA cold food storage guidance, grouped so you can scan straight to what you need.

## First, the rule: keep it at 40°F (4°C) or below

Every time below assumes a refrigerator running at **40°F (4°C) or colder** ([FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts)). A warmer fridge shortens all of them. If you only change one thing after reading this, check your fridge thermostat.

## Meat & poultry (raw)

| Food | Fridge (40°F / 4°C) |
| --- | --- |
| Ground meat & hamburger (beef, turkey, veal, pork, lamb) | 1–2 days |
| Fresh poultry, whole or pieces (incl. giblets) | 1–2 days |
| Fresh beef, veal, lamb, pork — steaks, chops, roasts | 3–5 days |
| Variety meats (liver, tongue, kidneys) | 1–2 days |
| Raw sausage | 1–2 days |
| Bacon | 7 days |

## Cured, smoked & deli meats

| Food | Fridge (40°F / 4°C) |
| --- | --- |
| Smoked breakfast links or patties | 7 days |
| Hard sausage (pepperoni, jerky sticks) | 2–3 weeks |
| Summer sausage labeled "Keep Refrigerated" | opened 3 weeks / unopened 3 months |
| Hot dogs | opened 1 week / unopened 2 weeks |
| Luncheon / deli meat | opened 3–5 days / unopened 2 weeks |
| Ham, fully cooked | whole 7 days / half 3–5 days / slices 3–4 days |

## Seafood

| Food | Fridge (40°F / 4°C) |
| --- | --- |
| Lean fish (cod, flounder, haddock, sole) | 1–2 days |
| Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, bluefish) | 1–2 days |
| Cooked fish | 3–4 days |
| Smoked fish | 14 days |
| Shrimp, scallops, squid, shucked shellfish | 1–2 days |
| Live clams, mussels, crab, lobster, oysters | 2–3 days |
| Cooked shellfish | 3–4 days |

| Food | Fridge (40°F / 4°C) |

| Buttermilk | 7–14 days |
| Yogurt | 7–14 days |
| Sour cream | 7–21 days |
| Cottage cheese | 1 week |

| Soft cheese (Brie, Bel Paese) | 1 week |

## The date on the package: quality, not safety

Here is the part that surprises people: in the US, product dating is **not required by federal regulation** for anything except infant formula. Those "sell by" and "best by" dates are about **quality**, not a safety deadline ([USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/food-product-dating)). The storage times above are the more useful guide.

One more habit that matters more than any date: as a general rule, don't leave perishable food out at room temperature for more than about two hours.
