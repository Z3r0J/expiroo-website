Food waste statistics are easy to quote and easy to get wrong. Here are the figures that hold up, with sources: how much food is thrown away, what it costs, and who does most of the throwing.

## The global picture: 1.05 billion tonnes

In 2022 the world wasted an estimated **1.05 billion tonnes** of food — about **19% of the food available to consumers** — according to the [UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024](https://www.unep.org/resources/publication/food-waste-index-report-2024), which drew on data from 93 countries. That works out to roughly **79 kg per person** every year.

## Households are the biggest source

Split by sector, households throw away the most by far: **631 million tonnes (60%)**, ahead of food service (290 Mt) and retail (131 Mt).

A common mistake to avoid: you may see UNEP's 2021 estimate held up against the 2024 figure as evidence that waste is climbing. UNEP itself warns the two **are not comparable** because the methodology changed, which is why this article shows no UNEP time series.

## What it costs

In the United States, the USDA estimates that **30–40% of the food supply** goes to waste ([USDA](https://www.usda.gov/about-food/food-safety/food-loss-and-waste)). The bill lands at home: the EPA calculates that a family of four loses about **$2,913 a year** — roughly **$728 per person**, or about **11% of a typical food budget** ([EPA, April 2025](https://www.epa.gov/land-research/estimating-cost-food-waste-american-consumers)).

## The UK picture

In the UK, WRAP puts total food waste at about **10.7 million tonnes in 2021**, with households responsible for roughly **60% (about 6.4 Mt)** — and about **67% of that was edible**. The average household throws away around **£470 of food a year** ([WRAP](https://wrap.ngo)).

## Where the biggest share hides

Every source lands on the same conclusion: the largest and most avoidable share of food waste happens in ordinary kitchens, not in shops or restaurants. That is the number a household can actually move, and it is the reason [Expiroo](/en) exists — a reminder before food goes bad, so less of it reaches the bin.
