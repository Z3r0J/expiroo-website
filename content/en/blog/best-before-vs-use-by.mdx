Best before vs. use by: the two dates sit in the same spot on the label and get read as the same thing, but they are not. One is a quality guideline you can override with your own judgement; the other is a safety limit you shouldn't push. Mix them up and you either bin good food or keep risky food a day too long.

## The use-by date is the safety line

A use-by date is the important one. As the UK's Food Standards Agency puts it: *"Use-by dates on food labels tell you when the food is no longer safe to eat."* And bluntly: *"Eating food after the use-by date could make you ill"* ([FSA](https://www.food.gov.uk)). You'll find it on the most perishable foods — meat and ready-to-eat salads, for example. Don't eat these after the date, even if they look fine.

## Best before is only about quality

A best-before date is about quality, not safety: *"The best before date tells you when the food might start to reduce in quality,"* and *"after the best before date, the food is usually safe to eat but may not be of the same quality"* ([FSA](https://www.gov.uk/understanding-food-labelling)). It appears on frozen, dried, canned foods and hard cheese.

| | Use-by | Best before |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Means** | Safety | Quality |
| **After the date** | Do not eat — risk of illness | Usually still safe; quality may drop |
| **Typical foods** | Meat, ready-to-eat salads | Frozen, dried, canned, cheese |

## You can freeze to beat a use-by date

The FSA confirms a useful workaround: you can **freeze food right up to the use-by date**. At −18°C food doesn't deteriorate, so freezing effectively pauses the clock. Once you thaw it, treat it as fresh and eat it within 24 hours.

## In the US, dating is mostly voluntary

American labels work differently. The USDA is explicit: *"Except for infant formula, product dating is not required by Federal regulations."* Those voluntary dates signal **quality**, which is why the FSIS recommends the phrase "Best if Used By" — it *"indicates when a product will be of best flavor or quality. It is not a purchase or safety date"* ([USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/food-product-dating)). The one exception is **infant formula**, which carries a mandatory Use-By date under FDA rules.
